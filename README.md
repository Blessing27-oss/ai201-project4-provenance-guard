# Provenance Guard

A backend system that creative sharing platforms can plug into to classify submitted content as AI-generated or human-written, score confidence in that classification, surface a transparency label to users, and handle appeals from creators who believe they've been misclassified.

---

## How It Works

Content submitted to `POST /submit` passes through two independent detection signals. Their scores are combined into a single confidence score, which is mapped to a transparency label. Every decision — signals, scores, and label — is written to an audit log. The raw confidence score is always returned in the API response for platform/developer use; it is intentionally not shown in the label copy shown to end users.

### Submission Flow

```
POST /submit
(raw text)
    │
    ▼
Rate Limiter (Flask-Limiter)
    │
    ├──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    ▼                                              ▼
Signal 1: Stylometry                     Signal 2: Groq LLM
(sentence length variance,               (semantic / stylistic
 type-token ratio,                        coherence judgment)
 punctuation density)
    │ stylometric_score                       │ llm_score / verdict
    └──────────────┬──────────────────────────┘
                   ▼
           Combine Scores
           → confidence_score (0.6 × stylometry + 0.4 × Groq)
                   │
                   ▼
           Map to Transparency Label
           (Likely AI / Uncertain / Likely Human)
                   │
                   ▼
           Write to Audit Log
           (signals, scores, label)
                   │
                   ▼
           Response: { result, confidence, label }
```

### Appeal Flow

```
POST /appeal
(content_id, reasoning)
    │
    ▼
Log appeal alongside original decision
    │
    ▼
Update content status → "under review"
    │
    ▼
Response: { status: "under review" }
```

## Detection Signals

### Signal 1 — Stylometric Analysis

Measures statistical surface properties that differ between human and AI writing:

- **Sentence length variance** — AI output tends to be more uniform; human writing varies more
- **Type-token ratio** — measures lexical diversity (unique words / total words)
- **Punctuation density** — frequency of punctuation relative to text length

The three metrics are normalized and combined into a single `stylometric_score` on a 0–1 scale.

### Signal 2 — Groq LLM Judgment

A Groq LLM call assesses whether the text reads as human or AI-generated by evaluating semantic and stylistic coherence holistically. It returns a verdict and a confidence value, converted to the same 0–1 AI-likelihood scale.

### Score Combination

```
confidence_score = (0.6 × stylometric_score) + (0.4 × groq_score)
```

Stylometry carries slightly more weight as the objective, deterministic signal. When the two signals disagree, the combined score trends toward the middle — this is by design, as genuine disagreement reflects genuine uncertainty.

## Transparency Labels

| Confidence Score | Label                                                                                          |
|-----------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 0.65 – 1.00     | "This content shows strong signals of AI generation."                                         |
| 0.35 – 0.65     | "We could not confidently determine whether this content is AI-generated or human-written. Mixed signals were detected." |
| 0.00 – 0.35     | "This content shows strong signals of human authorship."                                      |

---

## Signal Blind Spots

**Groq holistic judgment** can mistake a human's sophisticated or unusual vocabulary for an AI "trying too hard," since LLMs are trained partly on other AI outputs that skew toward polished word choice.

**Stylometric heuristics** can mistake a disciplined human writer — technical writers, formal essayists, some poets — for AI-generated text, because the heuristic measures variance and can't tell *why* variance is low.

### Known Edge Cases

**Non-native / ESL writing** — Non-native English speakers often use simpler sentence structures, more repetition, and less lexical variety than fluent native speakers. These are the same surface properties the stylometric signal associates with AI text, which can produce a false "Likely AI" or "Uncertain" result.

**Heavily human-edited AI output** — A creator who generates a first draft with AI and then substantially rewrites it may produce text that reads as natural to both signals. The system could confidently label AI-origin content as "Likely Human," missing the AI origin entirely.

---

## Appeals

When a creator believes their content was misclassified, they submit `POST /appeal` with their `content_id` and written reasoning. The appeal is logged alongside the original detection decision and the content status is flipped to `"under review"`. Appeals do not automatically override the original label; they surface the case for human review.

A false positive typically produces a borderline confidence score (roughly 0.55–0.65) rather than a high one, because a genuinely ambiguous case is what produces mixed signals in the first place. The "Uncertain" label is designed to communicate this to users upfront.
